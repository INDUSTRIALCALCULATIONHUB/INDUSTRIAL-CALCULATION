Engineering Basis and Practical Application

Dilute Phase and Dense Phase Pneumatic Conveying must be assessed in the context of the complete system, not as an isolated component. A useful basis includes solid properties, conveying mode, air supply, pressure, pickup velocity, route geometry, bends, airlocks and receiver filtration. Electrical decisions require current single-line information, documented protection philosophy, isolation control and competent electrical review.

Define the physical and operating boundary before selecting equipment, interpreting a result or changing a set point. Consider start-up, normal operation, maximum duty, low-load operation, upset condition, maintenance, seasonal variation and credible future changes. One of these cases can govern capacity, reliability, safety, product quality, emission performance or serviceability.

Major system technical extension

Pneumatic conveying engineering review

The system boundary starts at the material pickup or feeder and ends at the receiver or process inlet. It includes blower or compressor capacity, air treatment, feed vessel or rotary valve, pipeline, bends, boosters where used, receiver, vent filter, controls and dust discharge.

The material should be characterised for particle size, density, moisture, fragility, abrasiveness, permeability and flow behaviour. Those properties can change during conveying due to attrition, segregation, temperature or moisture exposure, so a single nominal product description is inadequate.

Route design must consider horizontal runs, vertical lift, bends, branches, dead legs, clean-out access, expansion, support and wear. A small routing change may materially affect pressure requirement, velocity, blockage risk and maintenance burden.

Control performance should be reviewed during filling, normal conveying, low-rate operation, line clearing, loss of air, receiver blockage and restart after a trip. Interlocks should prevent unsafe opening, overpressure, uncontrolled dust release or equipment start against a blocked condition.

Important trends include feed rate, conveying pressure, air flow, blower power, vessel cycles, receiver differential pressure, product breakage, bend thickness and blockages. Compare the same route and material condition before deciding whether the system has lost capacity.

Common corrective actions such as raising air pressure or velocity can increase wear and product damage. Analyse the gas/solids balance, pickup condition, feed stability, pressure loss and receiver performance before making such a change.

Commissioning needs representative material, calibrated instruments, a controlled route, defined test cases and criteria for flow, pressure, product quality, noise, leakage, dust and reliability. Retain the test evidence for later troubleshooting.

Pneumatic lines can retain pressure and material after shutdown. Isolation, depressurisation, cleaning and blockage clearing require written procedures, appropriate PPE and competent supervision. This guide does not replace hazardous-area, pressure-system or dust-hazard review.

Major-system final depth extension

Pneumatic-conveying troubleshooting, testing and lifecycle control

A systematic investigation begins by locating the restriction or instability. Compare feed consistency, air supply, line pressure profile, bends, receiver performance and downstream venting at the actual production rate. Do not assume the problem is in the longest line section; pickup, feed vessel, bend clusters and receiver filters are frequent controls on stability.

The solids-to-gas ratio changes through the route and can vary from one material batch to another. Changes in fines, moisture, bulk density, particle shape or degradation may shift the system from stable operation to deposition or excessive velocity. Representative testing and controlled trials are more reliable than extrapolating from a different material or a short trial route.

Pressure transmitters at selected route points, air-flow measurement, feed-rate records and receiver differential pressure provide a usable diagnostic picture. Measurements should be located and logged consistently. A single pressure reading cannot distinguish a restriction, high solids loading, loss of air, a worn bend or an outlet condition.

Wear management is a design and availability issue. High-velocity sections, bends, diverters and restriction points require accessible inspection and an agreed replacement strategy. Product that is fragile may require lower velocity or different routing; increasing velocity to clear a blockage can increase attrition and downstream dust.

Interfaces with storage and feeding determine whether solids are introduced consistently. A rotary valve, pressure vessel, screw feeder or hopper can cause surging, air leakage or unstable pickup even when the pipeline was correctly sized. Check the material extraction pattern and feeder controls before altering the transport air.

Energy performance depends on air generation, losses, leakage, filter condition, conveying velocity and duty cycle. Optimisation must not reduce reliability or create line deposition. Demonstrate a change over representative conditions and retain results for operations and maintenance.

Review process safety, pressure relief, isolation, static electricity, dust containment, noise, access, blocked-line procedures and emergency response. These requirements are project-specific and need qualified engineering and site approval.

Acceptance criteria should distinguish capacity, efficiency, quality, reliability, pressure loss, energy, emissions and safety. A system can meet one criterion while failing another. For example, an operational change that improves a local reading may increase energy consumption, wear, dust leakage, reagent use, product degradation or maintenance exposure. Translate data into maintenance action

Condition monitoring should connect a measured change to a practical response. Establish the normal trend, alert level, investigation trigger, responsible role and required evidence. Retain baseline measurements after commissioning and after major maintenance so future observations have a valid reference.

Critical spares should be selected from the system’s credible failure modes and repair time: consider components that can stop production, reduce environmental performance, create a safety constraint or have a long supply lead time. Storage, preservation, identification and the ability to fit the spare safely are part of the reliability plan. An unused spare without a compatible installation record may not reduce recovery time.

The review should identify affected drawings, data sheets, operating procedures, limits, permits, training, alarms, spare parts and emergency response. Topic-specific technical extension

Conveying mode and material response

Dilute and dense phase are operating approaches, not fixed equipment labels. The selected mode must suit the material’s particle size, fragility, moisture, abrasion, pickup behaviour, conveying distance and required rate range.

Develop the route with air supply, feed vessel or airlock, pipeline diameter, bends, receiver, vent filter and discharge arrangement as one pressure system. Start-up, shutdown and low-rate conditions need attention because instability or deposits can form before normal flow is established.

Use representative solids and inspect particle degradation, bend wear, air leakage, receiver pressure drop and line-clearing performance. A design that meets throughput but damages product or blocks during an upset does not meet the real duty.

Major system extension

Pneumatic conveying system design and operating envelope

Pneumatic conveying couples gas flow, pressure and solids behaviour. Dilute-phase systems generally rely on higher gas velocity to suspend and transport particles; dense-phase systems use lower velocity and higher pressure with controlled plug or strand movement. Neither label is sufficient for selection without representative material testing and route-specific design.

Capacity is affected by material bulk density, particle size, attrition, moisture, pickup conditions, conveying distance, elevation, bend layout, air supply, pressure vessel or feeder performance, receiver filtration and discharge. A change in one element can shift the operating point and change degradation, blockage, air demand or product breakage.

Excess velocity can increase wear, attrition and energy use; insufficient velocity can lead to deposition or line blockage. Check start-up, shutdown, empty-line clearing, low-rate operation, bend wear, airlock leakage, filter pressure drop, receiver venting and safe access for clearing. Do not open a pressurised or blocked system without an approved isolation and depressurisation procedure.

Useful operating evidence includes air flow and pressure profile, conveying cycle time, feed rate, receiver differential pressure, power, product degradation, bend condition and blockage location. Compare this information at a repeatable material condition and route configuration before changing pressure or velocity.

Integrated performance review

Major systems should be reviewed with a balance of process duty, equipment condition, controls, utilities, maintenance evidence, safety safeguards, waste or by-product handling and emission or product requirements.
