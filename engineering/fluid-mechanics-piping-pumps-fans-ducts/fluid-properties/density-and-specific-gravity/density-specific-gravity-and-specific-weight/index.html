Density, Specific Gravity and Specific Weight: Principles, Formulae and Industrial Applications

Density is mass per unit volume, specific gravity compares a stated density with a reference density, and specific weight is weight force per unit volume. These properties support fluid, bulk-solid and preliminary equipment calculations when their condition basis is declared.

What Is Density, Specific Gravity and Specific Weight?

Density is mass divided by volume. Specific gravity is a dimensionless density ratio against a stated reference, and specific weight is density multiplied by local gravitational acceleration. The terms are related but are not interchangeable.

Why Is Density, Specific Gravity and Specific Weight Important in Engineering?

These properties connect volume, mass, hydrostatic pressure, pump power and inventory. They are also used when interpreting bulk density, liquid transfer and gas-flow conditions.

Use the stated basis.State phase, temperature, pressure, composition and whether the value is true, apparent or bulk density. Specific gravity additionally requires a stated reference substance and condition.

Key Terms and Definitions

Mass, m
Amount of matter; kg.
Volume, V
Occupied space; m³.
Density, ρ
Mass per unit volume; kg/m³.
Specific weight, γ
Weight force per unit volume; N/m³.

Fundamental Principle

At a defined condition, a denser material contains more mass in the same volume. Specific gravity scales density against a reference, while specific weight translates density into gravitational force per unit volume.

Formulae, Symbols and Units

Density

ρ = m / V

Use mass m in kg and volume V in m³ to obtain density in kg/m³.

Specific gravity

SG = ρ / ρref

Use compatible density units and state the reference substance and conditions.

Specific weight

γ = ρ g

Use density in kg/m³ and gravity in m/s² to obtain N/m³.

Unit consistency

Do not mix mass and force units. Specific gravity has no unit; density and specific weight do have units and must be reported with the condition basis.

Assumptions and Validity Range

  • Mass and volume represent a defined, homogeneous sample or a clearly defined bulk state.
  • The selected gravity value is suitable for the intended preliminary calculation.
  • For gases and mixtures, temperature, pressure and composition are explicitly stated.

Factors Affecting Density, Specific Gravity and Specific Weight

Temperature

Temperature can change volume and therefore density.

Pressure

Gas density is particularly pressure-sensitive; liquids may also be affected in some services.

Composition and voids

Mixture concentration, entrained gas and bulk-solid void fraction can change the reported value.

Types, Classifications or Operating Cases

  • True density of a homogeneous substance.
  • Bulk density of particulate solids including void space.
  • Specific gravity against a stated reference fluid.

Step-by-Step Engineering Method

  1. Define the property required and the material condition.
  2. Use approved mass, volume and condition data.
  3. Calculate density, ratio or specific weight using compatible SI units.
  4. State the reference basis for specific gravity.
  5. Check whether process, storage or hydraulic work needs a more specific property definition.

Illustrative Engineering Example

Hypothetical example — not a design calculation

A liquid sample has a known mass and measured volume. The purpose is to calculate density, then express its ratio to a stated reference density.

  1. Divide sample mass by sample volume to obtain density.
  2. Divide that density by the stated reference density to obtain a dimensionless specific gravity.
  3. If weight force per volume is needed, multiply density by the stated gravity basis.

The calculation does not replace laboratory property data for a specified product composition or operating condition.

Industrial Applications

  • Mass-flow conversion and material balances.
  • Tank and vessel inventory estimates.
  • Hydrostatic pressure and pump-power calculations.
  • Bulk-solid storage and conveying capacity review.
  • Air and gas-system property assessment.

Common Mistakes and Limitations

Do not extend a preliminary relation beyond its basis.A handbook density without condition or material basis can be misleading. Verify the appropriate property definition before using it in design or safety work.
  • Giving specific gravity a unit.
  • Using bulk density where true density is required.
  • Omitting the reference condition for specific gravity.
  • Confusing specific weight with mass density.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does specific gravity have a unit?

No. It is a ratio of compatible densities.

What is the difference between density and specific weight?

Density is mass per volume; specific weight is gravitational force per volume.

Why can density change?

Temperature, pressure, phase and composition can change a material’s mass-to-volume relationship.
