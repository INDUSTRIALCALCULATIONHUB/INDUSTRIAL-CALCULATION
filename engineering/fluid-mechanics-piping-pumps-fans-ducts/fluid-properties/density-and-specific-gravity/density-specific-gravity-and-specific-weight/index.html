Density, Specific Gravity and Specific Weight

Density describes mass per unit volume. Specific gravity compares a substance with a stated reference density, while specific weight expresses weight force per unit volume. Together, these properties support fluid-system calculations, material handling and preliminary equipment evaluation.

What are density, specific gravity and specific weight?

Density is the mass contained in a unit volume of a substance. For a homogeneous sample, it is found by dividing mass by volume. It is an intensive property: at stated conditions, it does not depend on how much of the substance is present.

Specific gravity is a ratio. It compares the density of a substance with the density of a clearly stated reference substance at stated conditions. For many liquid engineering problems, water is used as the reference; for gases, the reference and conditions must be stated explicitly. Specific gravity has no unit.

Specific weight is the gravitational force acting on a unit volume. It is related to density by the local acceleration due to gravity. It is sometimes called weight density; it is not the same as mass density.

Why are these properties important in engineering?

Fluid density affects mass flow rate, static pressure change with elevation, pump hydraulic power, vessel inventory and buoyancy. Specific gravity is often used as a compact screening value for liquid handling, but it is useful only when its reference density and temperature basis are known. Specific weight is useful where a fluid column produces a force or pressure gradient.

Use the conditions with the value.A density or specific-gravity value without temperature, pressure, composition and reference definition may be unsuitable for engineering use.

Key terms and definitions

Mass, m
Amount of matter in a sample. SI unit: kilogram (kg).
Volume, V
Space occupied by a sample. SI unit: cubic metre (m³).
Density, ρ
Mass per unit volume. SI unit: kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³).
Reference density, ρref
Density selected as the comparison basis for specific gravity, at stated conditions.
Specific gravity, SG
Ratio of a substance density to its stated reference density. It is dimensionless.
Specific weight, γ
Weight force per unit volume. SI unit: newton per cubic metre (N/m³).
Gravitational acceleration, g
Local acceleration due to gravity. A commonly used preliminary value is 9.81 m/s², subject to project basis.

Fundamental principle

For an equal volume, the sample with greater mass has greater density. In a gravitational field, its weight force is also greater. Density therefore connects material inventory to volumetric flow, and specific weight connects density to the force produced by that inventory.

Specific gravity does not create a new physical property. It is a scaled comparison of density. It is meaningful only if the reference substance and its reference conditions are named.

Formulae, symbols and units

Density

ρ = m / V

Use mass m in kg and volume V in m³ to obtain density ρ in kg/m³.

Specific gravity

SG = ρ / ρref

Use compatible density units for both values. The result is dimensionless. State the reference substance and conditions.

Specific weight

γ = ρg

Use density ρ in kg/m³ and g in m/s² to obtain specific weight γ in N/m³.

Unit consistency

Do not mix mass units with force units. A kilogram is a unit of mass; a newton is a unit of force. When using density in kg/m³ and gravity in m/s², the product has units kg·m/(m³·s²), which is equivalent to N/m³.

Assumptions and validity range

  • The simple relations above assume the sample is described by a representative, defined mass and volume.
  • For compressible fluids, density can change materially with pressure, temperature and composition. Use the correct operating or design condition.
  • For mixtures, slurries, aerated liquids and bulk solids, the reported density must identify whether it is true, apparent, bulk or another defined density.
  • Specific-gravity values must not be used without a stated reference substance and reference condition.
  • Use project-specific gravity and property data for detailed design, statutory work, custody transfer, safety analysis or final equipment selection.

Factors affecting density

Temperature

Many substances change volume as temperature changes. A fixed mass can therefore have a different density at a different temperature.

Pressure

Gas density is especially sensitive to pressure. Liquids are often less sensitive, but pressure effects may still matter in high-pressure systems.

Composition

Mixture concentration, dissolved material, entrained gas and moisture can change a measured or calculated density.

Physical state

Solid, liquid and vapour phases have different density behaviour. State the phase represented by the data.

Types and operating cases

Engineers commonly encounter the following descriptions. The correct one depends on the system boundary and measurement method.

  • Liquid density: used for storage, pumping, pipe hydraulics and hydrostatic pressure.
  • Gas density: used for ventilation, combustion, compression and gas-flow calculations; always state pressure and temperature.
  • Bulk density: mass of a bulk solid divided by the space it occupies, including voids between particles.
  • Relative density: another term commonly used for a density ratio; confirm the definition used by the source or project.

Step-by-step engineering method

  1. Define the material and condition. Record phase, composition, temperature, pressure and whether a value represents true or bulk density.
  2. Choose the required property. Use density for mass–volume conversion, specific gravity for a stated comparison, or specific weight for a gravity-force basis.
  3. Use consistent SI units. Convert mass, volume and force data before substitution.
  4. State the reference. For specific gravity, record the reference material and its density or reference condition.
  5. Check the result. Confirm that the value has the expected unit and is reasonable for the stated material and condition.
  6. Escalate when necessary. Use approved project data and a qualified reviewer for final design decisions.

Illustrative engineering example

Hypothetical example — not property data

A liquid sample has a mass of 850 kg and occupies 1.00 m³. For comparison only, take the stated reference density as 1,000 kg/m³. Use g = 9.81 m/s².

  1. Density: ρ = m / V = 850 kg / 1.00 m³ = 850 kg/m³.
  2. Specific gravity against the stated reference: SG = 850 / 1,000 = 0.85.
  3. Specific weight: γ = 850 × 9.81 = 8,338.5 N/m³ ≈ 8.34 kN/m³.

The result is illustrative only. Actual fluid properties depend on material composition and the required operating condition.

Industrial applications

  • Converting volumetric flow rate to mass flow rate in process and utility systems.
  • Estimating static pressure variation in liquid columns and pipe systems.
  • Establishing preliminary inventory mass for tanks, vessels and sumps.
  • Reviewing hydraulic power inputs for pumping systems using the appropriate operating density.
  • Understanding gas-flow behaviour in fans, ducts, combustion systems and air-pollution-control equipment.
  • Describing bulk-solid storage and conveying capacities when bulk density is correctly defined.

Common mistakes and limitations

Do not treat a handbook value as universal.Material properties may be reported at reference conditions that differ from the system being evaluated. Confirm the source, condition, basis and uncertainty before use.
  • Calling specific gravity a density and assigning it a unit.
  • Using “water” as a reference without identifying the reference condition.
  • Confusing mass density with specific weight or using kilogram-force in an SI calculation without a clear basis.
  • Using gas density at one pressure and temperature for another operating condition.
  • Using bulk density where true particle density is required, or the reverse.

Frequently asked questions

Does specific gravity have a unit?

No. It is a ratio of two compatible density values and is therefore dimensionless. The reference substance and reference conditions still need to be stated.

What is the difference between density and specific weight?

Density is mass per unit volume, normally kg/m³. Specific weight is gravitational force per unit volume, normally N/m³. They are related by γ = ρg.

Can density be used to calculate mass flow rate?

Yes. When density represents the required system condition, mass flow rate can be obtained from mass flow rate = density × volumetric flow rate. Confirm the process basis before relying on the result.

Why must temperature be stated?

Temperature can change volume and therefore density. This can be significant for gases and can also matter for liquids, mixtures and reference-data comparisons.
