What Is Centrifugal Pump Working Principle?

A centrifugal pump transfers energy to liquid by rotating an impeller, then converting part of the resulting velocity into pressure in the casing or diffuser. Its duty depends on pump geometry, speed, liquid properties and the connected system.

Why Is It Important in Engineering?

Liquid enters near the impeller eye and is accelerated by rotating blades. The casing, volute or diffuser recovers pressure from velocity, while the system curve determines the actual operating point.

Use the stated basis.Identify the actual fluid, material, geometry, operating condition and relevant equipment boundary before using any engineering relation or reference value.

Key Terms and Definitions

Impeller
Rotating element that transfers energy to the liquid.
Volute or diffuser
Stationary passage that recovers pressure from velocity.
Head
Energy added per unit weight of liquid.
Operating point
Intersection of pump and system curves.

Fundamental Principle

Formulae, Symbols and Units

Hydraulic power

Pₕ = ρ g Q H

Hydraulic power depends on density, flow Q and differential head H.

Pump efficiency

η = Pₕ / Pshaft

Efficiency relates delivered hydraulic power to shaft power on a stated basis.

Unit consistency

Use a single, declared unit system. Confirm that all dimensions, properties and temperatures refer to the same service condition before combining them in a calculation.

Assumptions and Validity Range

  • The pump is properly primed and operates with adequate suction conditions.
  • The pump curve is valid for the installed impeller and speed.
  • The fluid is compatible with wetted materials and seals.

Factors Affecting the Result

Primary factor

Impeller diameter and rotational speed.

Service factor

Liquid density, viscosity and vapour-pressure behaviour.

System factor

System resistance and static head.

Step-by-Step Engineering Method

  1. Define required duty points and liquid properties.
  2. Select a pump family capable of the flow and head range.
  3. Compare the system curve with the applicable pump curve.
  4. Check NPSH, efficiency, power, control range and minimum-flow needs.
  5. Confirm materials, sealing, motor and installation details with the supplier.

Illustrative Engineering Example

Hypothetical example — not a design calculation

For a transfer system, construct a system curve from static head and losses, then identify the operating point on the vendor curve. Verify suction conditions and motor power before accepting the selection.

Use project data, applicable standards, supplier information and qualified review before making a design, procurement, construction or operating decision.

Industrial Applications

  • Water and utility transfer.
  • Process-liquid circulation.
  • Cooling and wash-water systems.
  • General industrial pumping.

Common Mistakes and Limitations

Do not extend a preliminary relation beyond its basis.Published equations and screening methods need appropriate geometry, operating data, source limits and independent engineering review for actual project use.
  • Selecting from a pump curve without a system curve.
  • Assuming rated duty equals installed duty.
  • Ignoring NPSH, viscosity or minimum-flow limits.
  • Using hydraulic power as motor input power without efficiency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a centrifugal pump pump any liquid?

No. Viscosity, solids, corrosion, vapour pressure and other properties affect suitability.

What sets the operating flow?

The interaction of the pump curve and the actual system curve.

Why is priming important?

Many centrifugal pumps need the casing and suction path filled to develop the intended liquid flow.
