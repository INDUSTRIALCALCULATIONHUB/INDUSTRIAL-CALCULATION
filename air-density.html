Engineering Knowledge

What is Air Density?

Air density is the mass of air contained in a unit volume and is one of the most important engineering properties used in HVAC systems, industrial ventilation, fan and blower selection, combustion systems, duct design, and Air Pollution Control (APC) equipment. Since air density varies with temperature, pressure, altitude, and humidity, engineers should always use actual operating conditions for accurate calculations.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter the installation altitude above mean sea level (m).
  2. Enter the actual air temperature (°C).
  3. The calculator automatically determines atmospheric pressure using the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA).
  4. The air density is calculated instantly in kg/m³.
  5. Use the calculated value for fan selection, airflow calculations, duct sizing, combustion analysis, and other engineering applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does air density decrease with altitude?

Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude, reducing the mass of air contained within a given volume and therefore lowering air density.

Does this calculator consider humidity?

No. This calculator assumes dry air and uses the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model for engineering calculations.

Can this calculator be used for HVAC design?

Yes. It is suitable for preliminary HVAC, industrial ventilation, fan selection, duct design, and process engineering calculations.

What is the standard density of air?

Under standard atmospheric conditions (15°C and 101.325 kPa), the density of dry air is approximately 1.225 kg/m³.
