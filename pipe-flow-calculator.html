Engineering knowledge

Continuity, pipe area and average velocity

For a steady incompressible flow, volume flow rate equals cross-sectional area multiplied by average fluid velocity. The relation is a useful first check for pipe sizing and velocity estimates, but it does not calculate pressure loss, pump head or compressible-flow effects.

Formula and inputs

Pipe area

A = πD² / 4

D is the internal pipe diameter in metres.

Flow rate

Q = A × V

V is average fluid velocity in metres per second.

Diameter

D = √(4Q / πV)

Use the result as a preliminary hydraulic sizing value.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The calculation assumes a circular pipe and an average velocity across its area.
  • It excludes friction loss, fittings, elevation, viscosity, density changes and transient effects.
  • Confirm nominal pipe size, schedule and actual bore from the applicable piping specification.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a pressure-drop calculation?

No. It only uses continuity. Add a suitable friction-loss calculation to determine pressure or head loss.

Which diameter should be entered?

Use the actual internal diameter, not simply the nominal pipe size.
