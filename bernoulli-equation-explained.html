Bernoulli Equation: Principles, Formulae and Industrial Applications

The Bernoulli equation expresses an energy balance for flowing fluid. It connects pressure, velocity and elevation heads, with pumps, turbines and losses included when the practical energy equation is used.

What Is the Bernoulli Equation?

The Bernoulli equation is a conservation-of-energy relationship written per unit weight of fluid. It compares two locations in a flowing system and is often used as a starting point for pressure, velocity and elevation calculations.

Why Is the Bernoulli Equation Important in Engineering?

It gives engineers a consistent way to connect flow conditions across pipes, ducts, nozzles, pumps and measurement devices. The equation must include real-system losses and energy additions or removals when those are significant.

Use the stated basis.Use the practical energy equation for real systems. State the points being compared, the fluid condition and all included pump, turbine and loss terms.

Key Terms and Definitions

Pressure head, p/(ρg)
Pressure energy expressed as an equivalent fluid-column height.
Velocity head, v²/(2g)
Kinetic-energy term based on mean flow velocity.
Elevation head, z
Potential-energy term relative to a selected datum.
Head loss, hL
Energy dissipated between the stated locations.

Fundamental Principle

For steady incompressible flow along a suitable streamline, the total of pressure head, velocity head and elevation head is conserved when no energy is added, removed or dissipated. Real systems require additional terms for those effects.

Formulae, Symbols and Units

Ideal Bernoulli equation

p₁/(ρg) + v₁²/(2g) + z₁ = p₂/(ρg) + v₂²/(2g) + z₂

Use compatible SI units: pressure Pa, density kg/m³, velocity m/s and elevation m.

Practical energy equation

H₁ + hP − hT − hL = H₂

Include pump head hP, turbine head hT and loss hL on a clearly defined system basis.

Unit consistency

Use one head basis throughout. Do not add pressure in Pa directly to elevation in m unless pressure is first converted to head using density and gravity.

Assumptions and Validity Range

  • The selected energy equation represents the actual system boundary and comparison points.
  • Velocity represents an appropriate mean velocity for the selected section.
  • Density is suitably constant, or compressible-flow effects are addressed separately.

Factors Affecting the Bernoulli Equation

Friction and fittings

Pipe roughness, length, valves and fittings create losses that must be included in practical work.

Pumps and turbines

Machines add or remove head and cannot be omitted from the energy balance.

Compressibility

Large density changes require a compressible-flow method rather than the simple incompressible form.

Types, Classifications or Operating Cases

  • Ideal streamline comparison for conceptual learning.
  • Pipe-system energy balance including head loss.
  • Pump-system balance using a pump curve and system curve.

Step-by-Step Engineering Method

  1. Define the two points, datum and flow direction.
  2. List pressure, elevation, area and flow data at each point.
  3. Calculate mean velocity from flow rate and area where appropriate.
  4. Add pump, turbine and loss terms on a consistent head basis.
  5. Check units, signs and whether the assumptions are appropriate.

Illustrative Engineering Example

Hypothetical example — not a design calculation

A pipe section changes elevation and diameter between two measured locations. The objective is to relate the observed pressure difference to velocity change and estimated losses.

  1. Choose the pressure tap locations as points 1 and 2 and select one elevation datum.
  2. Obtain velocities from the same flow rate and the two cross-sectional areas.
  3. Write the practical energy equation and retain the head-loss term rather than assuming an ideal system.

The illustrative method does not determine a design pressure drop without valid flow, geometry, fluid-property and loss data.

Industrial Applications

  • Preliminary pipe and duct pressure-balance calculations.
  • Pump head and system-curve interpretation.
  • Venturi and differential-pressure flow-measurement concepts.
  • Nozzle, diffuser and flow-area-change studies.
  • Hydraulic troubleshooting with measured pressure and elevation data.

Common Mistakes and Limitations

Do not extend a preliminary relation beyond its basis.Bernoulli’s equation is an energy balance, not a shortcut that removes the need to account for friction, fittings, rotating equipment or compressibility.
  • Comparing pressure values without considering elevation.
  • Omitting head loss in a real pipe system.
  • Using incompatible areas and velocities.
  • Applying incompressible assumptions to a gas system with material density change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Bernoulli apply to a pump?

Yes, in practical form when the pump head and system losses are explicitly included.

Is pressure always lower when velocity is higher?

Not necessarily. Elevation change, machine head and losses also affect the pressure relationship.

What is a head-loss term?

It represents energy dissipated by friction and local disturbances between the chosen points.
