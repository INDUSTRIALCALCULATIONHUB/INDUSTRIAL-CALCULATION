Bernoulli Equation: Formula, Assumptions & Engineering Applications

Understand Bernoulli's Equation, its derivation, assumptions, practical energy equation, engineering applications, limitations and real-world examples used in fluid mechanics and industrial piping systems.

Last Updated: July 26, 2026

What is the Bernoulli Equation?

The Bernoulli Equation is one of the most important equations in fluid mechanics and hydraulic engineering. It is based on the principle of conservation of mechanical energy and describes how pressure, velocity and elevation are related for a flowing fluid.

Whenever fluid accelerates, decelerates or changes elevation, energy is continuously converted between pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy. Under ideal flow conditions, the total mechanical energy remains constant along a streamline.

The equation is widely used in the design and analysis of pipelines, pump systems, HVAC duct networks, flow measurement devices, cooling water systems, compressed air systems and numerous industrial fluid applications.

Bernoulli Equation (Ideal Form)

For steady, incompressible and frictionless flow:

  • P/ρg + V²/2g + z = Constant

Where:

  • P = Pressure (Pa)
  • ρ = Fluid Density (kg/m³)
  • V = Fluid Velocity (m/s)
  • g = Acceleration due to gravity (9.81 m/s²)
  • z = Elevation above reference level (m)

Each term represents energy per unit weight of the fluid.

Meaning of Each Energy Term

  • Pressure Head (P/ρg) – Energy stored because of fluid pressure.
  • Velocity Head (V²/2g) – Kinetic energy due to fluid motion.
  • Elevation Head (z) – Potential energy because of height.

As velocity increases, pressure generally decreases if elevation remains constant. This relationship explains many common engineering phenomena such as nozzle flow, Venturi meters and pump discharge behaviour.

Assumptions of Bernoulli Equation

  • Steady flow
  • Incompressible fluid
  • Flow along a streamline
  • Negligible friction losses
  • No shaft work between sections
  • No heat transfer
  • No energy losses due to turbulence

When these assumptions are not fully satisfied, engineers use the practical energy equation with pump head and friction losses.

Practical Bernoulli Equation

Real engineering systems always experience friction losses and often include pumps or turbines. The practical energy equation therefore becomes:

  • P₁/ρg + V₁²/2g + z₁ + Hp = P₂/ρg + V₂²/2g + z₂ + Hf + Ht

Where:

  • Hp = Pump head added
  • Ht = Turbine head extracted
  • Hf = Friction and minor head losses

This equation forms the basis of hydraulic design for industrial piping and pumping systems.

Engineering Applications

Bernoulli's Equation is applied throughout mechanical, process and civil engineering wherever fluids are transported or measured.

  • Pipeline hydraulic analysis
  • Pump system design
  • HVAC duct pressure calculations
  • Cooling water circulation systems
  • Boiler feed water systems
  • Fire water networks
  • Venturi meters
  • Orifice meters
  • Nozzles and diffusers
  • Industrial process piping
  • Compressed air distribution
  • Water treatment plants

Worked Engineering Example

Water flows through a horizontal pipeline where the velocity increases from 2 m/s to 5 m/s.

According to Bernoulli's principle, the increase in velocity results in a decrease in pressure, assuming friction losses are negligible.

This principle is widely used in Venturi flow meters and pipe contractions where fluid acceleration produces a measurable pressure difference.

Importance in Engineering Design

Bernoulli's Equation helps engineers predict pressure variation, determine pump head requirements, evaluate system energy balance and understand fluid behaviour during design and troubleshooting. It is one of the first equations applied before performing detailed hydraulic calculations.

Limitations

  • Not suitable for highly viscous flows without loss calculations.
  • Compressibility effects become important at high gas velocities.
  • Cannot accurately predict turbulent energy losses by itself.
  • Applicable only along a streamline unless flow is irrotational.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does pressure decrease when velocity increases?

The total mechanical energy remains nearly constant. Therefore, an increase in kinetic energy (velocity head) is accompanied by a reduction in pressure energy under ideal flow conditions.

Can Bernoulli's Equation be used for gases?

Yes, but only when gas density remains approximately constant. For high-speed compressible flows, compressible flow equations should be used.

Is friction included in Bernoulli's Equation?

The ideal equation neglects friction. Practical engineering calculations include head losses using Darcy-Weisbach or Hazen-Williams equations.

Where is Bernoulli's Equation used?

It is widely used in pump systems, piping design, HVAC ducts, flow meters, nozzles, cooling water systems, compressed air systems and industrial process plants.

Conclusion

Bernoulli's Equation is one of the most fundamental tools in fluid mechanics. Although idealized, it provides the foundation for understanding fluid energy conversion and forms the basis for the hydraulic design of pipelines, pumps, ducts and flow measurement systems. When combined with head loss calculations and equipment performance data, it becomes an indispensable tool for practical engineering analysis.
